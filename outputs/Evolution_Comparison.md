**A guaranteed outright win over all opponents and all seats is impossible.** Rivals can submit the same program. In a four-copy game at most one player can finish strictly first, so at least three copies fail the proposed guarantee. Averaging over the four seats, any common policy's outright-first probability is at most 25%, including when the policy randomises. Equal-score ties may give several players shared credit, but do not produce four outright winners. This is not a 25% cap against different, weaker rivals. Rational players can optimise for their own score in different ways and respond to each other's policies. The useful objective is therefore strong measured tournament performance across maps and self-interested responses, backed by correct execution and failure recovery. No finite experiment can test every algorithm or certify an unknown final opponent population.

Both economic finalists also retain a limited final-turn safeguard. The wider planner and shield model remain heuristic and deterministic, and a rival that knows the code and reconstructs its memory can predict its decisions. The response search measures some such weaknesses without eliminating them all.

**Two memory defects and two scoring defects were reproduced and repaired in isolated copies.**

1. The original tracker treated any home-only starting layout as a new game, even after one or several genuine turns of staying or an opening detour. After five all-home stays it still reported turn 1 and zero scores, against true turn 6 and eight points each. The repair uses an 80-turn boundary or irreversible board evidence. Returning to the original positions during an opening preserves the game clock.
2. When the server replaced a requested SHIELD with STAY, the original tracker could insist that the shield happened, invent its own cooldown and miss a rival's actual shield. The repair uses current legal shield availability and frozen status to constrain what actually executed. The tested example restores the rival cooldown of four and the correct own progress.
3. Work gains and completion pruning omitted income earned on the completion turn. In a constructed final-turn neutral 3-point case, the original chose NORTH instead of a scoring STAY and gave up three points. The corrected planner includes the completion turn in captures, upgrades and candidate pruning.
4. Defence counted a visitor on an untakeable home as a takeover threat. In a constructed final-turn case it shielded that visitor while standing on a rival's ordinary 3-point cell, giving up its own 24-point raid bonus and three income. The corrected version excludes home visitors from takeover valuation and chooses the scoring stay in that case.
