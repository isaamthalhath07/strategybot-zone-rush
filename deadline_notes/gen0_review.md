## What the numerical changes might mean

**Bchild versus original B.** The exact changes are `BlockerBot.LEADER_BUMP:0.15→0.132626` and `_Core.HOP_RISK:0.45→0.455327`. The first reduces extra leader-focused denial. Under pairwise utility, attacking a distant leader can help other rivals while sacrificing our closer score boundary, so a smaller premium has a coherent strategic rationale. The second raises the threshold for hopping off rival land: it can allow more continued work at a narrowly borderline estimated Shield risk. It does not mean the bot hops more often. Its actual activation frequency is unknown here.

The child/original point difference is only 0.017090, with95% interval[−0.039551,0.068848] and adjusted interval[−0.076172,0.093262]. Field differences are−0.007813 economic,+0.033203 independent,+0.015625 erratic,+0.027344 pressure. This is consistent with a useful tradeoff but does not identify either gene as beneficial. Both changed together, opponent realizations differ, and no action/target/Shield event attribution was examined. The child retains the original observer, so its rank does not refute the f5 correctness fix.

**Bchild versus e8.** The overall point gain 0.045898 is not resolved: adjusted interval[−0.082031,0.184570]. Pressure contributes 0.142578/4=0.035645 of that mean difference; the other three field deltas are small positives. Original B already exceeds e8 in pressure by 0.115234. Therefore the architecture/reference contrast, not just the two numeric changes, accounts algebraically for much of that observed pattern. This decomposition is arithmetic, not a causal allocation of the gain to mechanisms. Do not tune a pressure-only switch from it.

**f5 versus original B.** The fixed memory correction has point gain 0.007324,95% interval[−0.057129,0.077148], adjusted interval[−0.089844,0.116211]. Its field deltas are−0.025391 economic,+0.035156 independent,+0.035156 erratic,−0.015625 pressure. The source-level repair of publicly contradicted Shield inference remains valid, but GEN0 does not resolve a strength benefit. Correct memory can interact with old heuristic weights, and a small overall difference can be obscured by lineup noise. Neither a guaranteed gain nor equivalence follows.

**Fchild versus original F.** The child changes `_Core.KEEP:0.8→0.713951` and `_Core.AGG0:0.35→0.346853`. Lower KEEP retains an existing target even when its value is further below the current best: it creates more commitment, not more frequent replanning. The aggression-prior reduction is small. Extra commitment can preserve an intentional deceptive route, but can also keep the bot on an obsolete or congested plan. The observed child delta is−0.067383;95% [−0.125977,−0.014160], adjusted[−0.164063,0.014648]. All four point field deltas are negative, with the largest loss in independent. That is a useful warning about this particular mutation, not proof that target retention caused its loss or that original Feint is robustly superior after adjustment.

**Learned8d versus e8.** Its `cash_weight:1.25275→1.047314` reduces the weight on immediate raid bonus, and `stay_prior:0.69484→0.655883` changes its initial estimate of rival staying. Its overall delta is−0.007324, adjusted[−0.112793,0.092285]. The independent-field point gain 0.089844 is offset especially by economic−0.134766. The old stage4 and GEN0 raw means face different maps/rival draws and cannot be used as a time-series strength trend.

Pressure is positive while the other three point field deltas are negative. The old winner has not been shown inferior here, and no incomplete architecture-study mean was consulted.

## Why field patterns do not identify the mechanism

The frozen field compositions are economic=(planner,planner,farmer), independent=(racer,blocker,raider), erratic=(wanderer,mixed,planner), pressure=(interceptor,feinter,research). Independent has the lowest observed means for most arms; pressure/erratic are often much higher. "Pressure" is a stratum name, not a proven ordering of adversary difficulty. Different algorithms, parameter draws and interactions can explain the pattern. Behavioral rivals include deliberately imperfect presence-based Shield responses, which can be baited; this is a source-based hypothesis about exploitability, not an observed explanation of the child win.

The deployed actor receives no field label. A policy that routes on `fixture.field` would violate the public-input boundary. Online adaptation may use public observations, but this panel does not authorize adding a field detector or fitting one to final outcomes. Likewise, aggregate rank means cannot reveal how often a changed hop threshold, denial bonus or target-retention test actually changed an action. A same-opponent causal intervention or preregistered mechanism trace would address a different question; none was executed for this review.

## Implications under the frozen protocol

Use the point ranking only for the predeclared TRAIN parent/elite rules. The leading source was selected from eight noisy estimates; its mean can include winner's-curse optimism. Fresh disjoint maps in later generations retest survivors, but repeatedly used rival algorithms still limit generalization. Sixteen map clusters and independent arm lineups explain why a fairly visible raw ranking can coexist with unresolved contrasts.1,024 games per arm are not1,024 map units. The source-frozen 28-pair report prevents selective omission; it does not create exact bootstrap coverage or a global best-policy certificate.

The registered same-generation f5 versus fixed-goal comparison remains the appropriate GEN1 structural test. These GEN0 results do not justify altering its hazard, moved factor, source bytes, fields, cases, stopping rule or control. Do not retroactively replace the disappointing child, combine the two leading sources, or select only the favorable pressure/independent subset. Wait for whole-generation root admission before examining any GEN1 result. Later DEV and untouched FINAL, with the fixed anchor and conservative rule, still determine delivery eligibility.
