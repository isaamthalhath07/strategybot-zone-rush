# How the candidates adapt during a game

Final selection is pending. The candidate families below make decisions from public observations and their own game history. Their forecasts can be useful without revealing what an opponent actually intends to do.

**What they can see.** Each callback supplies the board, the bot's seat, whether it holds the priority token, and legal actions. Public cells reveal ownership, occupant, base value, upgrade, home and frozen status. The policies reconstruct or estimate scores, work progress and Shield cooldowns from successive boards and their own accepted actions. They receive no rival code, type, seed, private state or future actions. Memory starts afresh each game.

**The original and evolved e8 planners.** These compare claiming, upgrading and raiding jobs using expected income lifetime, capture bonuses, travel, work, danger and onward routes. They learn from rivals staying on enemy land and responding with Shield, and use cooldown possibilities to assess raid windows. Rival score projections change denial weights: damage to a close competitor can matter more to pairwise rank. Evolved versions change bounded numeric priorities while retaining this planning logic. Their job forecasts approximate the future.

**Blocker and the public-memory Blocker.** The planner estimates rival targets and next steps from cell value, distance, occupancy and recent movement. Blocker adds pressure on the rival with the highest projected finishing score, favoring continuity in that leader choice. It can value taking and fortifying a wanted cell, occupying a route, or contesting a predicted next step when ownership or the token favors it. Public history updates its estimates of Shield use, raid persistence and mobility. These remain uncertain predictions.

Original Blocker can misattribute a rival's backfired Shield to a landowner, inventing a cooldown and suppressing real own raid progress. Original Feint shares this older observer. The f5 correction checks visible movement, ownership before work, known own actions and observed catches; blocked moves count as effective STAY. It repairs demonstrated memory failures while retaining the planner. Ambiguous rival Shield events still use a heuristic estimate. Correct memory alone does not prove stronger play.

**Feint.** Among nearly tied plans, Feint favors targets its simple observer model finds less obvious. It can approach a decoy while progressing toward its real target, prefer paths on a Shield-prone rival's land, or hop away when catch risk is high. Successful movement escapes a STAY-only catch; an occupied destination can block it. This may mislead a rival or bait a wasted Shield. The opponent may also ignore the decoy, decline to Shield or change plans.

**Why an edge move may be sensible.** A richer central cell is attractive, but travel, contests and repeated raids can make a quieter route or upgrade more productive. Retained income is roughly cell value × upgrade multiplier × useful turns; capture bonuses and damage to competitors also matter. Edges can still be poor choices through low values, fewer onward routes or costly detours. An observed edge move alone does not establish the planner's reason or that it was best.

**What remains unproved.** A separate persistent-goal candidate retained target scores across observations. Its controlled GEN1 comparison with f5 did not establish a gain; uncertainty allowed benefit or harm. Stationarity can mean working, a blocked move or freezing, and even a correct forecast can yield a poor rank decision. TRAIN comparisons do not establish optimality, superiority against unknown algorithms or a final winner. Untouched validation and registered release checks still govern delivery.
